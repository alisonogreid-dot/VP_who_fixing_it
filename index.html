02The Model Case: Fosen

Your teacher uses Fosen to show every step this week. Fosen is the class model, not your topic. You'll research your own Week 3 topic.

  • 2016–2020Europe's largest onshore wind farm (151 turbines at Storheia and Roan) is built on the Fosen peninsula in Trøndelag, on South Sámi winter reindeer pastures.
  • Oct 2021Norway's Supreme Court rules that the licences violated the herders' right to practise their culture (Article 27, UN International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights).
  • 2021–2023The turbines keep turning. In 2023, Sámi youth and supporters protest in Oslo and block the entrances to government ministries.
  • Dec 2023 / Mar 2024Agreements are signed with both herding districts. The turbines stay; herders get yearly compensation and extra winter grazing land, and can refuse further operation when the licence period ends.
  • The clashLate winter (January to Easter), reindeer can only feed where wind has cleared the snow off lichen. Those same windy ridges are the best places for turbines.
  • The bottleneckOnly a small part of the winter range is usable. A herd can only be as big as its winter pasture can feed.
  • The rulingThe Supreme Court found the winter pastures near Storheia and Roan had "in practice been lost" to reindeer herding.
  • Why it's cultureSouth Sámi has fewer than 1,000 speakers. Reindeer herding keeps the language alive.

Week 3 link: the ridges above the treeline are alpine tundra. It's cold and windy, with a short growing season and low-growing lichens and mosses.

"The government failed." Too vague. Who failed? To do what?

"The Norwegian state granted wind licences before the Sámi rights question was settled, and did not stop the turbines after the Supreme Court ruled against them in 2021. This is a failure of enforcement."

Use your Week 2 vocabulary: enforcement (rules exist but no one makes people follow them) · binding (an agreement countries must follow) · jurisdiction (who is responsible, and the gaps where no one is) · capacity (a state lacks the money or staff to act).

05Is It Really Official? Tuesday · 5 min

Slot B in your dossier is Official: a government (including its agencies and courts), a UN agency, or a company speaking for itself. Four checks before it goes in:

OriginWho made it? Is the organisation named?
PurposeWhy? To inform, to ask for money, to defend itself?
DateWhen? Recent enough for your question?
EvidenceNumbers, fieldwork, named sources?

Quick check: Official or not?

Decide for each source. You'll get feedback straight away.

07Write Your Equity Brief Tuesday · 20 min

Write one paragraph (150–200 words) to a named UN agency or government body. Explain who is hurt and justify what they should do. It's practice for the Open Letter brief in the final paper.

🚫

Write it in a separate document, NOT in your dossier. Your dossier may not contain pre-written sentences for the op-ed. Submit the brief where your teacher tells you.

1 · Claim + angle
From the angle of [angle], I am writing to warn that…
2 · Evidence
According to [organisation], … ("Page Title").
3 · Governance failure
This happened because [who] failed to [what]. This is a failure of [Week 2 term].
4 · The ask
I urge [agency] to [specific action], because…

Sentence starters are optional. Drop them if you don't need them. "Do more" is not an ask: name who should act, what they should do, and why it would work.

To: Ministry of Energy, Norway From the angle of access to solutions, I am writing to warn that Norway's green transition is being paid for by the Sámi reindeer herders of Fosen. In 2021 Norway's Supreme Court ruled that the licences for 151 wind turbines on their winter pastures violated their right to practise their culture (Supreme Court of Norway). Yet the turbines kept running for more than two years, until compensation deals were signed in 2023 and 2024 ("Agreement between Nord-Fosen Siida and Roan Vind"). This happened because the state granted licences before the herders' rights were settled, and did not stop the turbines after the ruling. This is a failure of enforcement. I urge the Ministry to require Indigenous consent before any new energy licence is granted, because rights that are only enforced afterwards do not protect anyone.
